# Look-back engagement, simulated against the ICP

Date of the simulation: 7 November 2026. Read against [ICP.md](ICP.md), approved 1 November 2026. The people and the decisions are invented. No real account was read. A skeptical critic, written as a person who already ignores reminders, argues why each persona would skip the look-back. The critic is not a vote. The scores are.

A category is worth encouraging only when three things are true for most of its decisions:

- It recurs, so a look-back can teach the next call.
- It resolves within a few days to four weeks, and the outcome is something the person can point at.
- They acted on the AI's advice, or on the cheap test. A check they did not use is not a loop.

Anything else stays quiet. A nudge on a one-off life choice, or on a bulk of quick checks, is how someone turns records off.

## How a look-back fails

The critic uses the same five reasons. They show up in the tables as tags.

- **Wrong day.** The outcome was knowable on a named day. A reminder 14 days later is late, or it arrives before anything has happened.
- **No scoreboard.** There is nothing to point at. "Held up" would be a mood.
- **Already know.** The box score, the deploy, or the invoice already told them. A second app that asks again feels like homework.
- **Not a decision.** It was a check, a rewrite, or one of many small plans in a row.
- **Feels graded.** The question sounds like a verdict on them, not a note of what happened. They will not tap it, and they may stop saving records.

## The hypothesis

Working hypothesis: encourage product and code ship calls, tool vendor and hire picks, pricing and spend, and forecasts and weekly bets. Do not nudge one-off life choices or bulk quick checks.

Confirmed, with two cuts.

Ship calls, pricing and spend, near forecasts, and weekly bets clear all three bars. The people acted on the outside opinion, the call comes around again, and a number or a deploy exists inside four weeks.

Tool, vendor, and hire picks do not clear the bar as a class. They clear it when the decision names a check inside four weeks: the payroll run, the end of the trial, the first week, the mover on the 20th. A broker, a lease, and a fleet "was it worth it" do not resolve in time. Encourage the first group. Leave the second alone.

Rejected for a nudge: one-off life choices, and bulk or quick checks. The critic's strongest line is Lena's. One graded question on a private call can end record-keeping for the work calls too. Sam's afternoon is the other line. Nine offers in an hour teach him that records are noise.

The 14-day default is why the critic still wins on the categories we should encourage. Jordan's slate, Priya's 15th, Lena's Tuesday, and Andre's Friday are not 14 days out. A look-back on the wrong day is an ignored look-back. The date should be the decision's own resolution when the words name one, and 14 days only when they do not. The person can change that date, or drop the look-back, on any decision.

What not to build: a streak, a score of the person, a required question before the verdict, or an offer on a quick take or a burst of checks. The offer, when it exists, is one line after the verdict. The outcome, when it is due, is one tap.
