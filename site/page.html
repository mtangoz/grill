How it works

One decision in, a verdict and its tests out

  1. You say “grill this”

    Name the decision, and how sure you are. A plan, a price, a hire, a forecast.

  2. Claude writes it up

    The options, your reasons, your confidence and the best case against. You check it. Nothing leaves until you approve.

  3. An outside judge attacks it

    It must argue your side first, quote the words it targets, and give every challenge a test that would settle it.

  4. You get a verdict

    Plus the challenges, ranked, each with its falsifier. Pick the tests worth running, and log your call.

Holds Holds with conditions Weak Refuted

One fatal challenge refutes. Ten minor ones don't. “Holds” with nothing found is a real answer.

Set up

  1. Get a key. At openrouter.ai/keys, sign in, click Create key, and add $5 of credit. A grill costs about a cent.

Privacy

Who can see your decision

Grill has no server, no account and no tracking. The one thing that leaves your machine is the write-up you approve, and it goes only to the judge you chose.

WhoWhat they seeWhat stops more
ClaudeThe conversation you're already havingYour Claude privacy settings
OpenRouterThe approved write-up, in transit, and request metadataIt stores no prompts unless you opt in. Keep two settings off; the privacy notes say which
The judge's modelThe approved write-up, in transitGrill routes only to zero-data-retention endpoints, and never falls back
The quality checkOff unless you turn it on. Then Jev (TypeSafe) scores the masked write-up and reportAlso a zero-data-retention endpoint
Grill's makersNothingThere is nowhere for it to go
  • Keys never leave. A key or token in the write-up stops the grill before anything is sent.
  • Contact details are masked. Emails, phone numbers and card numbers become [email], [phone] and [card number].
  • One destination. The code you install can reach OpenRouter and nothing else, and uses no third-party packages.
  • Checkable builds. Releases are built from the public source, with checksums and a provenance attestation.

Read the privacy notes. Every rule above is pinned by a test.

Getting better

It learns from choices, never from your decisions

OpenRouter's router improves without reading prompts: it follows which models people choose for each task. Grill borrows that idea.

Quality

Twelve made-up decisions with planted flaws, sound plans and loaded questions. Jev, a decision model from TypeSafe, scores whether each falsifier is a real test.

Monthly, opt-in

Usefulness

After a grill you can share a few choices: was it worth engaging, and later, did the call come true. The form has no text boxes.

Questions

Before you start

Why not just ask Claude to critique it?

You worked the decision out with Claude, so its critique is correlated with your reasoning. A judge trained by another company has different blind spots. Grill keeps Claude models out of the judging, and says so in the report if a run ever lands on one.

What's a falsifier?

The cheapest test that would settle a doubt either way: an email to your waitlist, a two-week price test, a call with three customers. Grill makes the judge name one for every challenge, so you leave with things to do, not just objections.

What if the judge is wrong?

It will be, sometimes. It has to quote the words it attacks, and Grill checks those quotes against your write-up, so a made-up objection is flagged. The verdict is evidence to weigh. You decide.

Does Grill keep my decisions?

No. There's no Grill server. Your log, if you keep one with the weekly review, lives in your own Google Drive, Notion or notes.

What's the weekly review?

An optional ritual. Once a week Claude reads the tools you've connected, lists the decisions you made, asks you to put a number on each, grills the one that matters most, and brings back the ones whose results are in. Once a month you bet on how many of your calls will come true.
