Your privacy, in plain words.

The free tool has no account. Grill keeps a grill from it only when you send that one. Grill in chat keeps your account, a 15-minute encrypted copy of each grill that gets a verdict, and a log and counts of your use. It keeps a record of a decision only if you say yes, encrypted, and you can delete it. Here is what is sent and counted, in short. The full list, route by route, is on the page linked at the end.

Our four promises

  • Your decisions are never public. We never publish a decision, and we never tie one to your name in public. Not in examples, case studies, marketing or anywhere else.
  • No ads. Grill shows no ads and has no advertising partners.
  • We never sell your data. Not your decisions, your email or your usage. Not to anyone.
  • We never train AI on your decisions. Grill improves by practising on made-up examples. The AI services that judge your decision are asked, on every request, not to keep it or collect it (zero data retention). The quality check that follows carries no such flag. Your decisions, below, says what Grill relies on instead.

Your decisions

The reflection at the end of a grill stays in your notes. This website does not store it. What is shared is what your assistant sends in the Grill tool call: the decision and, at most, a few sentences of context. Grill tells it to leave out the rest of the conversation, your memories and unrelated personal details, and cannot check that it did. If you copy and paste, it goes to the AI you paste it into. With the one-click version, it goes to AI services that are asked not to keep it, plus a quality check by TypeSafe that’s on by default. That check request carries no zero-retention flag. Grill relies instead on TypeSafe being the only provider of that model on OpenRouter, a weekly check of its endpoints, and reading an answer only if TypeSafe served it. On the installed tool you can turn the check off in settings. In chat you skip it one grill at a time, by saying skip the check. Email addresses, phone numbers, card and bank numbers, ID numbers, labelled dates of birth, street addresses, postcodes and IP addresses are hidden before the decision goes to the model router. Grill’s server gets it as your assistant sent it, not masked, and what Grill keeps is not masked. Names are never hidden, so leave out or reword anyone you would not name in public. And Grill gets better by practicing on made-up examples, never on your decisions.

Accounts and keys

A Pro account, which is not live, is only for a key we manage. A starter allowance needs no card. Bring your own key and there is no account. We see your email, the assistant and judge you pick, and your key’s usage (cost and model, never the text). We count sign-ups, keys issued, first grills, allowances used up and upgrade clicks. Apart from the 15-minute encrypted copy of each grill in chat and a grill you send after a reaction, we don't keep what you decide unless you choose to keep decision records (Grill in chat) or saved report copies (Pro), and you can delete them.

Using Grill in chat (the connector)

Anyone can sign up and connect Grill to the AI they use at https://grillyour.ai/mcp.

  • Sign-in. You sign in with a code we email you. Use the same email you signed up with every time, and check spam if the code doesn't arrive. There's no password. Clerk runs the sign-in for us and keeps your email and the sign-in event.
  • Your decision. The decision your assistant sends passes through Grill's server to reach the judge. It's not logged, except that a /judge link carries it in the web address. It is kept for 180 days only if you later send that grill, and a record is kept only if you choose.
  • A page, when the assistant cannot connect. /judge shows the decision from the link and sends it to the judge only when you press the button. The link itself carries the decision, so opening it delivers it to Grill's server and the host's request log, and it stays in your browser's history. If you have to sign in first, the address with the decision in it goes to Clerk. It is the same judge, the same caps, and the same choice about records. A decision over 4000 characters is not sent. If the link names the assistant (`from=gemini`, `gemini-cli`, `antigravity`, `Google-Discovery-Engine`, `claude`, `chatgpt`, `grok`, `muse` or `meta`), the judge is not from that company. With no name Grill knows, nobody is ruled out, and the page and the verdict say independence was not verified.
  • The verdict. The decision and the verdict are held encrypted for 15 minutes, records on or off, so your AI can fetch the verdict and a reaction can include them.
  • Records, only if you say yes. If you choose to keep records, a draft waits encrypted for up to 14 days for your call. It is deleted sooner if you turn records off or press Delete all. Your decision records are then kept encrypted. If you say no, no record is kept. The 15-minute copy, your account and the counts are kept either way.
  • You control them. See, export or delete any record, or all of them, at grillyour.ai/decisions. Deleting your account at grillyour.ai/account removes your records, drafts, reactions, your key and your sign-in. It does not lower any count, and your account ID stays in the Monday email lists until they expire.
  • Who can read them. Only you see your records on the site. Grill's maintainers hold the encryption keys, so they could technically decrypt records and drafts you chose to keep, the 15-minute copy of each grill, and reactions. A grill you choose to send can be read by them, so Grill can learn what is working. They don't publish it, and they don't read a grill you did not send.
  • What we count. We keep the cost, time and model of your last 400 grills, plus a monthly spend number for the free allowance. We also keep an anonymous count of how many people use Grill each day, week and month. That count can't list who was counted, and none of it includes the words of a decision. While Grill's list of its own test and maintainer addresses is set, Grill also adds one to a counter when you first grill, grill again later, answer the records question, or save a choice or a look-back. Those counters hold whole numbers by day or app, and no text or account ID.
  • The Monday email. It is on unless you turn it off. It goes on a Monday when your account saved a decision that week or a look-back came due. Resend sends it, with your email address, how many of that week's grills changed your call, and the first sentence of each decision whose look-back came due. To write it, a job opens kept records each Monday. One account in four is held out and gets no email, to test whether it helps. Lists of account IDs, of who was sent it, who was held out and who came back, are kept 180 days. Sending is set to stop after 25 January 2027.
  • An invite code. If you enter one, we check it and don't store it as you typed it. We keep a one-way hash of your email, a one-way hash of the code, and how many times that code has been used. Deleting your account does not remove that, so the same email can sign in again without using the code a second time.
  • The waitlist. If you join it, we keep the email you typed and the source you came from, in the same store as the account. We don't email you. You can sign up without joining. If a code is minted for that email later, we keep a one-way hash of a single-use invite code, not the code itself.
  • A founding member number. If you are one of the first 100 people let in, we keep a one-way hash of your email and the number it was given, plus a count of how many numbers have been given. We don't keep a list of the addresses. Deleting your account does not remove the number, so the same email keeps it.
  • Who else is involved. OpenRouter routes your decision to the judge, under zero data retention. The judge model's provider sees the masked decision under that same request. TypeSafe runs the quality check, with no zero-retention flag on that request. Clerk runs sign-in and keeps your email. Upstash stores what Grill keeps: records, drafts, the 15-minute copy and reactions are encrypted, and your account row, with your email address, and the counts are not. Resend sends the Monday email. Vercel hosts the site and Grill's server.

This website and the installed tool

This website counts visits anonymously, with no cookies and nothing that identifies you. /start sets no cookie while sign-up is open, as it is now. When sign-up needs an invitation, a /start link with a source tag sets one cookie holding that tag for 30 days. Signing in sets sign-in cookies. The installed tool can optionally send one anonymous usage ping to this site after the first successful grill, if you turn that on at install; off by default; never again; never decision words.

The same count can record that a decision record was shown or copied, that a look-back started or finished, and how many records were pasted. It can also record a click on a download, the paste prompt, or the OpenRouter keys page, and an optional channel tag (ref) from a fixed list. A missing tag is left off. It never includes the words of a decision.

Vercel, which hosts this site, does the counting. When Grill turns it on, PostHog counts the same things too, in a PostHog account that belongs to Grill alone. Neither one sets a cookie or saves anything in your browser, and neither builds a profile of you, records the page, or looks up where you are.
