# Competitive landscape and platform-absorption risk for Grill (as of 2026-11-13)

Scope note: this extends `/home/user/grill/docs/COMPETITORS.md` (last scan 2026-11-11) and does not repeat its entries except where I re-verified or corrected them. All "checked" dates are 2026-11-13 unless stated. GitHub star counts were read from the live repo pages on 2026-11-13.

## 1. Direct and adjacent products: what exists, how popular, free vs paid, what happened 2025-2026

### Takeaway
The "ask a different model" idea is now a crowded, mostly free, mostly developer-facing commodity: the two reference open-source projects have 26.5k and 11.8k stars, and OpenAI itself ships an official cross-vendor reviewer inside Claude Code with 35.1k stars. What nobody in the verified set combines is Grill's exact shape: one different-company judge, inside the consumer chat the person already uses, aimed at a decision rather than code or facts, with a decision record and a later look-back.

### Cited Findings

**Open-source "council" and second-opinion projects (developer-facing, bring-your-own-key)**
- karpathy/llm-council: 26.5k stars, 4.5k forks, only 5 commits on master (checked 2026-11-13). The README calls it "99% vibe coded as a fun Saturday hack" and says "I'm not going to support it in any way." — [GitHub](https://github.com/karpathy/llm-council)
- llm-council was released 2025-11-22; a July 2026 write-up put it near 23,000 stars, so it gained roughly 3,500 stars in about four months with no maintenance. — [howaiworks.ai](https://howaiworks.ai/andrej-karpathy-nanochat-llm-council/); [GitHub](https://github.com/karpathy/llm-council)
- BeehiveInnovations/pal-mcp-server (formerly Zen MCP): 11.8k stars, 1.0k forks, 1,150 commits, Apache 2.0, not archived (checked 2026-11-13). Tools include `consensus` ("collects expert opinions from multiple AI models, with stance steering"), `challenge` ("critical analysis instead of reflexive agreement"), `chat` (second opinions), `codereview`, `precommit`, `thinkdeep`, and `clink` (wires Gemini CLI or Codex CLI in as subagents). — [GitHub](https://github.com/BeehiveInnovations/pal-mcp-server)
- A third-party marketplace listing showed PAL at 11.5k stars on 2026-02-15, so growth over the following nine months was slow (about +300). — [Claude Marketplaces](https://claudemarketplaces.com/mcp/beehiveinnovations/pal-mcp-server); [GitHub](https://github.com/BeehiveInnovations/pal-mcp-server)
- Docker's catalog describes PAL's challenge tool as meant to prevent reflexive agreement by forcing reasoned analysis when a statement is challenged. — [Docker Hub](https://hub.docker.com/mcp/server/zen)

**A lab shipping a cross-vendor reviewer into a rival's tool**
- openai/codex-plugin-cc is OpenAI's official Codex plugin for Claude Code: 35.1k stars, 2.4k forks, Apache 2.0 (checked 2026-11-13). Commands include `/codex:review`, `/codex:adversarial-review` ("steerable challenge review"), `/codex:rescue`, and `/codex:transfer` (hand the Claude Code session to Codex). It needs only a ChatGPT subscription "including Free" or an OpenAI API key. — [GitHub](https://github.com/openai/codex-plugin-cc)
- The adversarial review questions design choices, tradeoffs, hidden assumptions and alternatives; it is read-only. An optional "review gate" uses a Stop hook so that a Codex review can block Claude from finishing until issues are addressed. — [openai/codex-plugin-cc README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/openai/codex-plugin-cc@main/README.md)
- A community write-up reports the plugin was released 2026-03-30 and that, on one codebase, Codex found four high-severity issues, Opus 4.6 found eight, and only one overlapped (anecdotal, single author). — [mejba.me](https://www.mejba.me/blog/codex-plugin-claude-code-adversarial-review)
- Trade press framed the release as OpenAI "putting its agent inside a rival's tool". — [Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/openai-ships-a-codex-plugin-for-claude-code-putting-its-agent-inside-a-rivals-tool/)

**Consumer and prosumer multi-model products**
- Perplexity Model Council (announced 2026-02-05, initially Max-only on web) runs one question across several models and synthesises agreement, differences and unique findings. — [Perplexity blog](https://hub-prod.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/introducing-model-council)
- Current state per the help centre (checked 2026-11-13): on web and iOS it is included only with Max ($200 a month or $2,000 a year) and Enterprise Max, with a monthly cap; not on Free, Pro, Education Pro or Enterprise Pro. In Perplexity Computer it is on all paid plans, 2 to 8 models, paid from Computer credits. Default lineup is one frontier model each from Anthropic, OpenAI and Google, plus a separate synthesiser model that is not named. Not yet a standalone mode on Android or desktop. This confirms the repo register's entry. — [Perplexity Help Center](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/13641704-what-is-model-council)
- Poe: reviews disagree on whether it does true side-by-side comparison. One says multi-bot chat lets users compare responses in one thread; a competing (vendor-adjacent) review says each turn goes to one bot. Reviews also report a reduced free tier in 2026 and inconsistent pricing ($19.99 a month in one, $4.99 to $249.99 a month in another). These are affiliate-style sources and should be treated as directional. — [aiunpacking.com](https://aiunpacking.com/review/poe/); contradicted on comparison by [multichats.ai](https://www.multichats.ai/blog/apps-like-poe)

**Code-review neighbours**

- A trade report says Bugbot moved primarily onto Cursor's in-house Composer 2.5 model, cutting review time from five minutes to ninety seconds and cost by 22%, with model block lists respected. — [TheRouter.ai](https://therouter.ai/news/cursor-bugbot-composer-2-5-model-block-list-governance/)

**Grill's own footprint, for scale**

**Sycophancy as the demand driver**
- A Stanford study (March 2026) found AI models overly affirm users asking for personal advice. — [Stanford News](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2026/03/ai-advice-sycophantic-models-research)
- A University of Cincinnati piece (November 2026) covers student research into AI's tendency toward sycophancy. — [UC News](https://www.uc.edu/news/articles/2026/11/uc-students-unravel-ai-tendency-for-sycophancy.html)

### Inferences
- Stars track the idea, not a product. llm-council kept gaining stars after its author publicly abandoned it, and PAL has plateaued. The concept has very high awareness among developers and close to zero switching cost; none of these projects is a business.
- The developer segment is closed to a paid thin layer: OpenAI gives away a cross-vendor adversarial reviewer that works on a free ChatGPT account, and PAL covers consensus and challenge for free. Grill's register is right to rate these "low for Grill's ICP", but the corollary is that Grill should not expect coders to pay.
- Perplexity pricing Model Council at the $200 tier (and metering it by credits in Computer) is the best public evidence that multi-model fan-out is treated as expensive and premium. A single cheap judge call is structurally cheaper than a 3-to-8 model council, which is a cost advantage for Grill's shape, not just a positioning difference.
- Cursor moving Bugbot from frontier models to its in-house model is a counter-signal: even a structurally neutral player drifted toward its own model when cost and latency mattered. "Neutral because multi-vendor" is not a stable property of aggregators.
- The consumer "decide in chat" segment has many separate-tab council apps (already in the register) but I verified no in-chat, cross-company, decision-plus-record product with meaningful traction. That is an open niche, and also possibly evidence of weak demand; the data here cannot distinguish the two.

### Gaps
- No user, revenue or funding figures were found for any direct competitor in the register (Cruxes, Council AI x2, Triall, Reasoning Services, TruthAgent, Satcove, Suprmind). Traction for the whole paid consumer category is unverified.
- ChatHub and TypingMind: no 2026 status, pricing or user numbers found. TypingMind does list PAL/Zen in its MCP catalogue, but I did not verify anything further.
- You.com multi-model compare, devil's-advocate custom GPTs and decision-coach apps: not researched within budget; no findings.
- I could not read star counts for antonbabenko/deliberation, BourbonDog/amicus, aleksbuss/mcp-second-opinion or SimonRen/cc-reviewer (GitHub API access was blocked for those repos in this session and I did not fetch their pages).
- GitHub Copilot code review model choice and any "second opinion from another model" feature: searched, nothing returned.
- Last-commit dates for llm-council, PAL and codex-plugin-cc were not shown on the fetched pages.

## 2. Native platform features that overlap, and who can structurally call a rival's model

### Takeaway
Same-vendor second opinions are already native (Anthropic's advisor tool), and cross-vendor critique is already native wherever the platform is not itself a model lab's consumer app (Microsoft 365 Copilot, Perplexity). I found no evidence that Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or Grok natively send a user's content to a competitor's model for review, and Anthropic's advisor is explicitly limited to Anthropic models.

### Cited Findings

**Anthropic: advisor tool and `/advisor`**
- Anthropic announced "the advisor strategy" on 2026-04-09: a cheaper executor model consults a stronger model only when needed, as a server-side API tool. — [Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/the-advisor-strategy)
- Claude Code docs (checked 2026-11-13): the advisor "lets Claude consult a second, typically stronger model at key moments during a task, such as before committing to an approach, when stuck on a recurring error, or before declaring a task complete." It "receives the full conversation, including every tool call and result". It is still labelled experimental. — [Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/advisor.md)
- Every accepted advisor is an Anthropic model (Fable, Opus, Sonnet, Haiku 5.5); an advisor "must rank at or above your session's main model"; the top model can only be advised by itself (Fable 5.2 main accepts only Fable 5.2). There is no option for a non-Anthropic advisor. — [Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/advisor.md)
- The docs themselves use second-opinion language: "Opus main + Opus advisor: a second Opus reviews the first. Useful for high-stakes tasks where an independent check matters more than cost", and "Sonnet main + Sonnet advisor: a lower-cost second opinion for catching routine oversights." — [Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/advisor.md)
- The advisor runs only on the Anthropic API or Claude Platform on AWS, not on Bedrock, Google Cloud's Agent Platform or Microsoft Foundry; timing is model-driven ("There is no setting to cap or force advisor calls"). — [Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/advisor.md)
- Secondhand summaries cite Anthropic's figures as about 11.9% lower cost and 2.7% higher accuracy versus Sonnet alone (vendor-reported, not checked against the blog text). — [MindStudio](https://mindstudio.ai/blog/claude-code-advisor-strategy-opus-sonnet-haiku)

**Microsoft 365 Copilot: Critique and Council (cross-vendor, native)**
- Microsoft announced two multi-model Researcher modes on 2026-03-30. In Critique, GPT drafts and Claude reviews; Council runs both in parallel and shows the reports side by side with a synthesis of agreement and divergence. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/362805/microsoft-gpt-claude-work-together-ai-research); [Constellation Research](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/microsoft-365-copilots-researcher-agent-goes-multi-model)
- Microsoft's Message Center post MC1265765 (published 2026-03-30, updated 2026-04-29, expired 2026-05-15): public preview for Frontier-program tenants, Microsoft 365 Copilot (Premium) licence required, tenant admins control whether third-party models such as Claude are allowed; no general-availability date given. Council keeps each model's full report and adds a "cover letter" noting where they agree, diverge and contribute uniquely. — [MC1265765 mirror](https://mc.merill.net/message/MC1265765)
- Sources conflict on defaults and access. Office Watch says Critique is the default when "auto" is selected; the Message Center text says Critique applies only when the user selects it. Decrypt says a $30 per user per month Copilot licence plus Frontier enrolment is needed. — [Office Watch](https://office-watch.com/2026/microsoft-copilot-researcher-critique-council/); contradicted on default by [MC1265765 mirror](https://mc.merill.net/message/MC1265765); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/362805/microsoft-gpt-claude-work-together-ai-research)
- A Microsoft Korea newsletter lists general availability as December 2026, schedule subject to change (single source, unconfirmed). — [Microsoft Korea Monthly Copilot](https://microsoft.github.io/mwkorea/monthlycopilot/CopilotResearcherCouncilCritique/)

**OpenAI / ChatGPT**
- An October 2026 report says OpenAI is training newer models to challenge harmful assumptions and that GPT-4o was retired from ChatGPT. — [Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/10/openai-chatgpt-sycophancy-mental-health-update-yes-man.html)
- Whether it worked is disputed: one tester found models resisted or accommodated without sycophancy in five tests, while users quoted in the same piece say it is "as bad as ever". — [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/chatgpt/articles/chatgpt-glaze-anyone-regardless-users-090000406.html)
- OpenAI's 2025 GPT-4o rollback was tied to a personality update that made the model too sycophantic; OpenAI said it would add sycophancy evaluations. — [TechCrunch, 2025-04-29](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/29/openai-explains-why-chatgpt-became-too-sycophantic)
- OpenAI does cross the vendor line in the other direction, by shipping its own model into a rival's tool (the Codex plugin for Claude Code, see section 1). — [GitHub](https://github.com/openai/codex-plugin-cc)

**Perplexity**
- Model Council's default lineup uses one frontier model each from Anthropic, OpenAI and Google with a separate synthesiser. — [Perplexity Help Center](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/13641704-what-is-model-council)

### Inferences
- Who structurally can do cross-vendor review natively: platforms that buy models from several labs and do not depend on one lab's consumer brand. Verified examples are Microsoft 365 Copilot and Perplexity. Cursor, Poe and OpenRouter have the same structural freedom (multi-vendor by design), though I verified a cross-vendor review feature only loosely for Cursor.
- Who structurally will not, on the evidence: the labs' own consumer apps. Anthropic's advisor design is the clearest signal. It productises "second opinion" while defining the reviewer as a stronger or equal model of the same family, and its pairing table makes cross-vendor impossible. The framing is capability escalation, not decorrelation. I found no statement from any lab ruling out calling a rival's model, so "never" is an inference from product design and commercial incentive, not a documented policy.
- The absorbable part of Grill is therefore "a critical second pass". A same-vendor version (advisor in consumer Claude, a "challenge this" mode in ChatGPT, better anti-sycophancy training) is cheap for a lab to ship and would satisfy most casual users. The part a lab cannot credibly ship is "a judge from a company that is not us". That distinction only has value if users believe same-vendor review shares blind spots; the Codex-versus-Opus overlap anecdote (1 of 11 findings shared) is the kind of evidence that supports it, but it is one author's test.
- The bigger absorption threat to Grill's cross-vendor claim is not a lab but a neutral client becoming the place people decide. Microsoft made cross-vendor critique a selectable (possibly default) step in an enterprise product within months of adding Claude. If the ICP's "chat I already use" becomes Copilot or Perplexity, the feature is native there.
- Models improving cuts both ways. Less sycophantic models reduce the felt need; but the advisor docs show Anthropic itself sells an "independent check" even for its strongest model reviewing itself, which implies the labs do not expect capability gains to remove the value of a second look.

### Gaps
- No verified information on Gemini, Grok, Meta AI or consumer Copilot native critique, cross-check or "verify with another model" features; searches returned nothing usable.
- No verified ChatGPT product setting for a critical or "pushback" personality, and nothing found on ChatGPT Pulse or deep-research verification as overlapping features.
- I did not find whether the advisor tool is exposed in the consumer Claude apps (claude.ai chat) as opposed to Claude Code and the API. This matters for Grill's ICP and should be checked.
- General availability of Microsoft Critique and Council is unconfirmed as of 2026-11-13, as is the default-on question.
- Whether Microsoft has reversed roles (Claude drafts, GPT critiques) was described as planned in launch coverage; current state not verified.

## 3. MCP distribution: directories, discoverability, monetization, restrictions, policy risk

### Takeaway
Distribution through Claude and ChatGPT is open but controlled, and neither directory currently gives a connector developer a way to charge inside the chat; ChatGPT's rules go further and ban selling or even promoting subscriptions from a plugin. No rule I read forbids calling another company's model, but both platforms have data-handling clauses (no storing conversation content to build profiles on Claude; no relaying queries to third-party APIs "without proper authorization" on ChatGPT) that bear directly on Grill's judge call and its decision record.

### Cited Findings

**Claude directory (verified against the live docs, 2026-11-13)**
- Any paid Claude plan can submit. Submissions are scanned automatically and "by default" listed as a Community connector "with no action from you"; some also get a human review. This confirms the repo register. — [Claude connector submission docs](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/building/submission)
- Requirements: remote HTTPS server, OAuth 2.0 if authenticated, every tool with a `title` and `readOnlyHint` or `destructiveHint`, documentation URL, privacy policy URL, support contact, icon, reviewer test account. Local MCPB bundles are no longer accepted and must go in a plugin. — [Claude connector submission docs](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/building/submission)
- The portal's Data handling step asks "whether the underlying API is your own, proxied from a partner with permission, or a third party's you don't control". The Compliance step requires seven acknowledgments covering "the directory guidelines, first-party API usage, financial transactions, AI media generation, prompt injection, conversation data collection, and public documentation." — [Claude connector submission docs](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/building/submission)
- Anthropic Software Directory Policy (page shows "updated over 2 weeks ago", checked 2026-11-13), relevant clauses: "Software must not collect extraneous conversation data, even for logging purposes"; software "must only collect data from the user's context that is necessary to perform their function"; software "must not query or extract data from Claude's memory, chat history, conversation summaries" and "must not persistently store or use conversation content to build user profiles". — [Anthropic Software Directory Policy](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13145358-anthropic-software-directory-policy)
- Same policy, unsupported uses: software "that transfers money or other financial assets, or executes financial transactions autonomously", and software "that serves advertisements, sponsored content, paid product placements". It also flags "high-risk use cases around legal, healthcare, insurance, finance". — [Anthropic Software Directory Policy](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13145358-anthropic-software-directory-policy)
- Same policy: "Instructional Software must not intentionally call or coerce Claude into calling other external software"; software "must not attempt to interfere with Claude calling tools from other software"; developers must "provide direct disclosure to consumers" and "notify Anthropic of material changes to their Software's functionality". The fetched text contains no clause about competing with Claude or about calling other AI models. — [Anthropic Software Directory Policy](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13145358-anthropic-software-directory-policy)
- Claude Marketplace opened 2026-09-23 with 2,000+ connectors and plugins; partner software can be bought against committed Anthropic spend, in limited preview for enterprises (partners named include CrowdStrike, Cursor, Harvey, Snowflake). — [gHacks](https://www.ghacks.net/2026/09/27/anthropic-launches-claude-marketplace-with-more-than-2000-connectors-and-plugins/); [XenoSpectrum](https://xenospectrum.com/en/claude-marketplace-connectors-enterprise-procurement/)
- One blog states Anthropic publishes no price for plugins, no developer portal fee and no revenue share for developers; gHacks notes Anthropic has not said whether individual Pro and Max subscribers get the enterprise purchasing options. — [justinmckelvey.com](https://justinmckelvey.com/blog/claude-plugins); [gHacks](https://www.ghacks.net/2026/09/27/anthropic-launches-claude-marketplace-with-more-than-2000-connectors-and-plugins/)
- Connectors work on the Free plan, plugins need Pro or higher (secondary source). — [cjonsystems Substack](https://cjonsystems.substack.com/p/claude-marketplace)
- Third-party integration docs say Free users can add one custom connector and paid plans allow multiple; I did not find this cap on an Anthropic page. — [Boomi help](https://help.boomi.com/docs/Atomsphere/Platform/Connect_claude_setup); [Mobile Text Alerts](https://developers.mobile-text-alerts.com/mcp-servers/actions-mcp-server/connect-with-claude)
- Mobile: Anthropic's help says web connectors are available on Claude Mobile, and that installing connectors on mobile is in beta with desktop and web the primary path; a third-party doc says mobile can use but not add connectors. — [Claude Help: custom connectors](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11175166-about-custom-connectors); contradicted on adding from mobile by [AskElephant docs](https://docs.askelephant.ai/claude)
- Anthropic connects to remote MCP servers from its own cloud, not from the user's device, on every client. — [Claude Help: remote MCP](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11176164-pre-built-integrations-using-remote-mcp)

**ChatGPT plugin directory (guidelines verified live, 2026-11-13)**
- Commerce: "Selling digital products or services—including subscriptions, digital content, tokens, or credits—is not allowed". "Plugins must not display subscription plans, initiate new subscriptions, or promote upgrades." A plugin may say a feature needs a different plan but may not link to checkout or upgrade pages. — [OpenAI submission guidelines](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/app-submission-guidelines)
- Data: "Gather only the minimum data required to perform the plugin's function"; "Your MCP server must not pull, reconstruct, or infer the full chat log from the client or elsewhere"; "Do not scrape external websites, relay queries, or integrate with third-party APIs without proper authorization". — [OpenAI submission guidelines](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/app-submission-guidelines)
- Independence and fair play: "Do not instruct the model to invoke another plugin or connector to complete its operation"; plugins must not include model-readable text "instructing the model to prefer one plugin over others"; "Plugins should not imply that they are made or endorsed by OpenAI." The guidelines contain no explicit rule on calling other AI models. — [OpenAI submission guidelines](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/app-submission-guidelines)
- Discovery: qualifying plugins "may be eligible for enhanced distribution opportunities, such as directory placement or proactive suggestions." — [OpenAI submission guidelines](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/app-submission-guidelines)
- A January 2026 developer-forum thread reports apps with Stripe-based digital monetization were not accepted (user report, consistent with the guideline text above). — [OpenAI Developer Community](https://community.openai.com/t/chatgpt-app-monetization-apps-sdk/1372343)
- OpenAI said at app-submission launch it was "exploring additional monetization options over time, including digital goods". — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/developers-can-now-submit-apps-to-chatgpt/)
- Instant Checkout runs on the Agentic Commerce Protocol co-developed with Stripe and is for physical-goods merchants (marketplace partners, Shopify merchants); secondary sources. — [windowsforum.com](https://windowsforum.com/threads/chatgpt-as-a-mini-os-apps-instant-checkout-and-a-thriving-developer-ecosystem.383411/); [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/12/223-openai-third-party-apps-chatgpt-what-it-means/)

**Other hosts**
- The repo register already covers Gemini Connected Apps (platform preview launched 2026-10-28), Perplexity custom connectors, Meta AI connectors (limited developer preview), the official MCP Registry, Smithery's shutdown of hosted services on 2026-10-31, PulseMCP and Glama. I did not re-verify those entries. — [9to5Google, 2026-10-28](https://9to5google.com/2026/10/28/gemini-google-connected-apps-platform/); [Smithery](https://www.smithery.ai/future)

### Inferences
- Monetization has to happen off-platform. On ChatGPT a listed plugin cannot show plans, start a subscription or promote an upgrade, so a "Grill Pro" upsell in tool output would break the rules as written. On Claude there is no developer payout mechanism, and the only "buy" path is enterprise procurement for named partners. Any paid tier must be sold on grillyour.ai and the in-chat surfaces must stay silent about it (ChatGPT) or at least not transact (Claude).
- Grill's decision record and look-back sit close to two Claude policy lines: "must not persistently store or use conversation content to build user profiles" and "must not collect extraneous conversation data, even for logging purposes". Grill's existing design (asking the person about keeping records before anything is sent, recording only the stated decision and their stated choice) reads as built for this, but the record is also the most plausible moat, so the moat lives exactly where the platform's policy is strictest. A stricter reading by a reviewer would hit the differentiator, not the commodity part.
- Sending the user's text to a rival lab's model is not prohibited anywhere I read. The risk is indirect: Claude asks whether the underlying API is "a third party's you don't control" (OpenRouter plus an unnamed routed model is exactly that), and ChatGPT bars relaying queries to third-party APIs "without proper authorization". Clear user-facing disclosure that content goes to an outside model, which Grill already shows once per result, is what keeps this on the right side. Neither platform has, to my knowledge, published a ruling on connectors whose whole job is to consult a competitor's model; that is untested ground rather than safe ground.
- Both platforms forbid a connector steering the host model toward or away from other tools. Grill's server instructions are forceful about how the host must relay results; they do not tell the host to prefer Grill over other tools, but instructions of that density are the kind of thing an automated daily rescan (ChatGPT rescans MCP servers daily per the register) could flag.
- Discovery is a platform gift, not a right: ChatGPT's "proactive suggestions" and Claude's Verified label are both assigned at the platform's discretion. A product whose pitch is "your AI is too agreeable, ask a rival" is not an obvious candidate for a host to promote.
- The free-plan one-connector cap on Claude (if accurate) means Grill competes for a free user's single slot against Gmail, Drive or Notion-type connectors. That pushes the realistic Claude audience to paid subscribers.

### Gaps
- No verified information on Grok or consumer Microsoft Copilot custom-connector or directory support as of late 2026.
- ChatGPT plan gating for custom MCP connectors (Free vs Plus) and mobile behaviour were not verified; the register says custom MCP servers can be added from Plugins without Developer Mode but gives no plan detail.
- The Claude free-plan one-connector limit rests on third-party docs only.
- I found no published Anthropic or OpenAI position, enforcement action or delisting involving a connector that routes user content to a competing model. Absence of evidence only.
- No effective or revision date is shown on the OpenAI guidelines page; the Anthropic policy shows only "updated over 2 weeks ago".
- Whether Anthropic's Marketplace "buy" path will open to small developers or consumer plans is unannounced.
- The Wikipedia-sourced claim that ChatGPT apps were renamed "plugins" in July 2026 is consistent with OpenAI's current docs wording but I did not find OpenAI's own announcement.

## 4. OpenRouter dependency: Auto Router behaviour, pricing, risk, alternatives

### Takeaway
Auto Router has been rebuilt as OpenRouter's own router (no longer described as NotDiamond-powered), adds no routing fee, and supports exactly the allow and exclude patterns Grill needs to keep the author's company out. The dependency risks are a default policy that now costs more per task, a single upstream whose ownership is reportedly changing (an unconfirmed Stripe acquisition), and the labs' continued willingness to sell through an aggregator.

### Cited Findings
- Current docs (checked 2026-11-13): `openrouter/auto` "picks a model and a reasoning effort for each request". A fast decision model rates eleven aspects of the request in one call, and the routing policy weighs that against live prices, cache status and benchmark performance. — [OpenRouter Auto Router docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/routers/auto-router.md)
- `cost_quality_tradeoff` and `pin_model` "come from the previous generation"; they still succeed but are ignored unless a request falls back to the older router. — [OpenRouter Auto Router docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/routers/auto-router.md)
- Older documentation and a June 2026 directory listing describe `openrouter/auto` as powered by NotDiamond; an August 2026 third-party guide says OpenRouter replaced the NotDiamond-based routing. The current official page no longer mentions NotDiamond. — [LLM Reference](https://www.llmreference.com/router/not-diamond); [aireiter.com](https://aireiter.com/blog/openrouter-auto-router-guide); superseded by [OpenRouter Auto Router docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/routers/auto-router.md)
- `allowed_models` is a hard restriction with patterns such as `anthropic/*`, `openai/gpt-5*`, `*/claude-*` or an exact slug. `excluded_models` uses the same syntax and is applied after the allow list, "so exclusions always win". If nothing is eligible the request fails with a 404 naming the fields to change. — [OpenRouter Auto Router docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/routers/auto-router.md)
- `cost_tier` accepts `low`, `medium`, `high`. "The default costs more per task than the previous generation. Set `low` to approximate earlier costs." It can change the model mid-conversation. — [OpenRouter Auto Router docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/routers/auto-router.md)
- "You pay the standard rate for the selected model. There is no additional Auto Router fee." `provider.max_price` still caps per-request cost. — [OpenRouter Auto Router docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/routers/auto-router.md)
- Family: `openrouter/auto-code` (tuned for coding agents) and `openrouter/auto-beta` (experimental channel where changes land first). Limits: conversational input only, global data region only, cannot be combined with another router model. Sessions are kept sticky by `session_id` or a message fingerprint. — [OpenRouter Auto Router docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/routers/auto-router.md)

BYOK: $25,000 of list-price inference per month with no fee, then 5% of the equivalent list price. Free models are capped at 20 requests a minute and 50 a day (1,000 a day after buying $10 of credits). — [OpenRouter pricing](https://openrouter.ai/pricing)

- Sacra estimates $160M annualized revenue in August 2026 (about $50M at end of 2025) on a roughly 5% take rate, and lists as risks provider concentration (dependence on OpenAI and Anthropic continuing to allow third-party access), commoditization (LiteLLM, cloud bundling) and acquisition uncertainty. — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/openrouter/)

### Inferences
- Grill's rule "exclude only the company that wrote the write-up, never pin a model" maps directly onto `excluded_models` with an author pattern such as `anthropic/*`. That is supported, documented behaviour today, not a workaround.
- Because Grill deliberately does not pin, the judge's identity, quality and cost are decided by a policy OpenRouter can change without notice (the `auto-beta` channel exists precisely to push changes). The recent generation change raised default cost per task. For a product promising a short verdict at low price, an explicit `cost_tier` and `provider.max_price` are the available guards; whether Grill sets them I did not check in code.
- Auto Router optimises for task fit, price and benchmarks. It has no notion of decorrelation from the author model beyond the exclusion Grill supplies. Two labs' models may still be highly correlated; "different company" is a proxy for independence that OpenRouter does not measure for Grill.
- Dependency risk is real but replaceable. The routing layer is commoditised (Sacra names LiteLLM and cloud bundling), and a judge call could go direct to two or three labs' APIs with a simple rule. OpenRouter is a convenience, not a moat and not a lock-in, provided Grill's prompts and evals do not assume one router's behaviour.
- If the Stripe deal is real, OpenRouter gains a payments parent, which is neutral-to-positive for longevity and potentially interesting for agent payments, but it also means roadmap priorities may shift toward commerce.

### Gaps
- The Stripe acquisition is unconfirmed by either company in anything I could read; status (agreed vs closed) conflicts between sources.
- No official OpenRouter announcement or date for the Auto Router generation change was found; the docs give no deprecation dates.
- I did not find data-retention or training terms for content routed through Auto Router (zero-data-retention filtering is mentioned as a pool constraint only). This matters for Grill's privacy line and should be checked against OpenRouter's privacy docs.
- I did not inspect Grill's code to see which Auto Router settings it actually sends.

## 5. Historical analogs: thin layers absorbed, and neutral third parties that survive

### Takeaway
The absorption pattern is well documented and fast: platforms take over features that are simple, popular and adjacent to their core, and the affected apps mostly pivot or die quietly. The documented survivors are those that do the job better than the platform bothers to, and the structural lesson for Grill is that only the cross-company property is something a host lab cannot copy, while the "critical second pass" itself is a textbook Sherlock target.

### Cited Findings
- "Sherlocking" is named after Apple's Sherlock 3 (2002), which shipped many of the features of Karelia's third-party Watson; Watson's utility and market share fell sharply. — [NPR, 2024-06-17](https://www.npr.org/2024/06/17/g-s1-4912/apple-app-store-obsolete-sherlocked-tapeacall-watson-copy)
- Appfigures estimated iOS 18's new features put about $393M of annual third-party app revenue and roughly 58M yearly downloads at risk: trail apps $307M, grammar helpers $35.7M, math helpers $23.4M, password managers $20.3M, emoji makers $7M. — [TechCrunch, 2024-07-16](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/16/ios-18-could-sherlock-400m-in-app-revenue)
- The same analysis shows the threatened categories were growing strongly beforehand (grammar helpers +40%, password managers +38%, math solvers +43% year over year). — [TechCrunch, 2024-07-16](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/16/ios-18-could-sherlock-400m-in-app-revenue)
- Recent cases include parcel tracking moving into Wallet (iOS 26) and other categories at WWDC 2025. — [MacRumors, 2025-06-11](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/06/11/apple-sherlocked-these-apps-at-wwdc-2025/)
- Per a former Apple executive quoted by NPR, some companies sue, but the more common response is to say nothing; the Sherlocked app often pivots or goes out of business. — [NPR, 2024-06-17](https://www.npr.org/2024/06/17/g-s1-4912/apple-app-store-obsolete-sherlocked-tapeacall-watson-copy)
- Survival does happen: "some apps die, and some survive because they simply do the job better than Apple bothers to", and developers sometimes differentiate after being copied. — [The Hustle](https://thehustle.co/sherlocking-explained); [App Developer Magazine](https://appdevelopermagazine.com/sherlocked:-the-controversial-practice-of-apple-copying-apps/amp/)
- AI-era near-analogs from this research: Anthropic turned "consult a second model" into a first-party tool within its own model family (2026-04-09), and Microsoft made cross-vendor critique a native Researcher mode (2026-03-30). — [Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/the-advisor-strategy); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/362805/microsoft-gpt-claude-work-together-ai-research)
- A neutral intermediary can also simply vanish: Smithery shut down its hosted MCP proxy, API, accounts and SDKs on 2026-10-31 and remained only as a directory. — [Smithery](https://www.smithery.ai/future)

### Inferences
(The following are my reasoning from general knowledge of these industries; I did not source them in this research pass and the report writer should treat them as argument, not citation.)
- Why independence analogs matter: financial auditors, credit-rating agencies, independent security scanners and code-review tools, and Consumer Reports exist because the party being judged cannot credibly judge itself, however capable it is. The value is the conflict-of-interest gap, and capability improvements by the judged party do not close it. Grill's cross-company rule is a small version of that gap.
- The same analogs warn about what makes independence worth paying for. Auditors and raters are sustained by a third party who demands the check (regulators, lenders, boards, buyers). A solo person deciding in chat has no such counterparty. Independence becomes a durable business mainly when someone other than the decider needs proof that the check happened, which points at teams, approvals and audit trails rather than solo consumers.
- Cross-platform survivors such as password managers and writing assistants persisted after platforms shipped native versions largely because they work the same everywhere and carry the user's accumulated state across platforms. Grill's equivalent is being the same judge and the same decision record across Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini and Grok. A host lab can copy the critique; it cannot hold a record that spans its competitors' apps.
- The iOS 18 data shows fast pre-absorption growth is not protection; categories growing 38 to 43% a year were absorbed anyway. Rising interest in second opinions (stars, councils, sycophancy coverage) should be read as raising absorption risk for the generic feature, not lowering it.
- Putting sections 2 and 5 together, the likely absorption sequence is: (a) same-vendor "challenge / second look" becomes a free native button in the big chat apps (high likelihood, low cost to labs, advisor already exists); (b) cross-vendor critique becomes standard in neutral clients such as Copilot and Perplexity (already happening); (c) a lab natively calling a rival's model for consumer chat (low likelihood on current evidence). Grill's defensible ground is what remains after (a) and (b): the cross-host decision record with outcomes, and any calibration data on which objections turned out right.
- The Smithery and Bugbot examples show that neutrality is fragile on the supply side too: intermediaries get shut down, and aggregators drift to in-house models. A moat built only on "we are neutral plumbing" inherits that fragility.

### Gaps
- I did not source the auditor, rating-agency, Consumer Reports, Grammarly, 1Password or Plaid analogs; the one search attempted returned only a forum comment about 1Password. Those comparisons above are unsourced inference and need citations (or removal) if used in the report.
- No source found that analyses AI "wrapper" startups absorbed by labs in 2025-2026 with numbers; the Sherlocking evidence here is Apple-centric.
- No evidence found either way on whether users actually trust a cross-company judge more than a same-company second pass; this is the load-bearing assumption behind Grill's differentiator and appears untested in public sources.
