Researcher note on evidence quality: primary sources were fetched for ForecastBench (FRI posts of Mar and Jul 2026), the Metaculus "11 analyses" roundup (Jul 2026), the Fatebook launch thread (Jul 2023) and the Fed SR 26-2 letter (Apr 2026). ## 1. Decision journals and personal forecasting: adoption, retention, monetization

### Takeaway
I found no published user counts, retention figures or revenue for any personal decision-journal or prediction-tracking product; the best-known ones (PredictionBook, Fatebook, Metaculus, Manifold) are charity-, grant- or donation-funded rather than subscription businesses. That absence is itself the main finding: after 15+ years the category has devoted fans but no demonstrated paying market.

### Cited Findings
- Fatebook (launched 11 Jul 2023 by Adam B / Sage Future, a 501(c)(3) charity) was positioned as "the fastest way to make and track predictions" and a successor to PredictionBook. The launch post and its 43 comments give no usage numbers, no pricing and no monetization model; the only money mentioned is a commenter's $1,000 donation. The founder later said he was "busy with various other projects" and not spending much time on it. — [LessWrong launch post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/yS3d46m23wRKDQobt/introducing-fatebook-the-fastest-way-to-make-and-track)
- In the same thread, the stated reason people left PredictionBook was friction ("clunky", "lots of friction"); Manifold was described as "intimidating" for personal logging. Fans said low friction is what made it stick ("as much a reflex and habit to me as Google Search"; "game changer ... how practical it is to weave predictions into my decisionmaking"). Requested friction-reducers: mobile app, non-Google sign-in, less bulky Slack output. A commenter's question "How has this been in the long run?" went unanswered; another asked what practical purpose such sites serve and got no clear answer. — [LessWrong launch post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/yS3d46m23wRKDQobt/introducing-fatebook-the-fastest-way-to-make-and-track)
- Fatebook's distribution is embedding into existing workflows: web, Slack, browser extension, plus a Beeminder integration. It gives Brier score, relative Brier score and a calibration chart as questions resolve. — [Fatebook EA Forum post](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/DWFRBzK3rAH3HFDZr/fatebook-the-fastest-way-to-make-and-track-predictions); [Beeminder help](https://help.beeminder.com/article/366-fatebook) (search summary)
- Manifold: an EA Forum critique says revenue from users buying mana was under $10k/month, probably not covering staff; Manifold tried real-money "sweepcash" and discontinued it in 2025; early funding came from FTX Future Fund and Survival and Flourishing Fund (search summary, low confidence on figures). — [EA Forum: "Manifold Markets isn't very good"](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/EaR9xFxspmYRkm3eo/manifold-markets-isn-t-very-good); [longtermwiki Manifold](https://www.longtermwiki.com/wiki/E546)
- Metaculus does not publish user counts or audited financials; revenue is described as externally funded tournaments plus a pro-forecaster service, and critics say it receives "millions of dollars per year" in grants (search summary). — [The State of Forecasting (forecasting.substack)](https://forecasting.substack.com/p/the-state-of-forecasting-dynamics); [LessWrong "Forecasting is Way Overrated, and We Should Stop Funding It"](https://greaterwrong.com/posts/WCutvyr9rr3cpF6hx/forecasting-is-way-overrated-and-we-should-stop-funding-it)
- Failure modes named in decision-journal guides (blogs, not data): entries written after the outcome is known become rationalised records; logging every minor decision turns the journal into "administrative clutter"; documentation overhead (search summary). — [Umbrex decision journal framework](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/decision-making-frameworks/decision-journal/); [reflect-os blog](https://reflect-os-website.pages.dev/blog/decision-journal)
- General subscription-app context (RevenueCat 2026 report as covered by press): AI apps convert trials better (median 8.5% vs 5.6%) and earn about 41% more per payer, but retain worse (12-month annual retention 21.1% vs 30.7%; monthly-plan retention 6.1% vs 9.5%). — [TechCrunch, 10 Mar 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/) (search summary)

### Inferences
- The category's revealed business model is philanthropy. Every durable personal forecasting tool I could trace is grant/donation funded, and the one that tried consumer monetization (Manifold) reportedly earned trivially. A paid "saved history + look-back reminders" tier would be attempting something no predecessor has demonstrably achieved.
- The consistent qualitative signal is that capture friction decides adoption: tools win when the prediction is recorded inside a workflow the person is already in (Slack, browser, chat). Grill's capture-at-the-moment-of-decision inside the AI chat is the strongest version of this seen so far, because the record is a by-product of getting a verdict rather than a separate journaling act.
- The unsolved half is the look-back. Delayed feedback (weeks to months), ego cost of recording a miss, and ambiguous outcomes are structural; none of the sources show a product that solved them. Expect the look-back completion rate, not the capture rate, to be the binding constraint on any calibration dataset.
- Audience skew: the proven users are rationalist/EA/forecasting hobbyists, a small niche, not general knowledge workers.

### Gaps
- No user counts, DAU/retention or revenue found for Fatebook, PredictionBook, Farnam Street's decision journal, or Annie Duke-derived tools. I found no first-hand Hacker News/Reddit post-mortems of abandoned decision journals in this session (the search returned none), so "why people quit" rests on blog assertions and the Fatebook thread.
- No data on what fraction of logged predictions ever get resolved by their authors.

## 2. Team/enterprise decision tools: Cloverpop, ADRs, "decision intelligence"

### Takeaway
The one venture-backed company that sold decision logging and outcome tracking to enterprises (Cloverpop) stayed small after roughly a decade and was absorbed in 2021; the "decision intelligence" label that Gartner formalised in 2026 is dominated by analytics/automation platforms (FICO, IBM, SAS, Quantexa, Aera), not human decision records. Engineering ADRs are the most widespread decision-record habit and even they show low, inconsistent adoption.

### Cited Findings
- Cloverpop was acquired by Clearbox Decisions on 27 Jul 2021; the Cloverpop brand and product team continued. Total funding figures conflict: PitchBook about $8.0M, CB Insights $17.48M including an unverified $3.42M raise dated May 2025. It still operates and is listed in Gartner's Decision Intelligence Platforms market. No later exit or shutdown found (search summary). — [Cloverpop blog](https://www.cloverpop.com/blog/cloverpop-was-acquired-by-clearbox-decisions); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/83291-77); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/cloverpop)
- Gartner published its inaugural Magic Quadrant for Decision Intelligence Platforms on 26 Jan 2026, defining the category as technology combining "decision modeling, analytics and AI to augment and automate decision-making". Leaders (per vendor releases): FICO, IBM, SAS, Quantexa, Aera; Decisions a Challenger; o9 and FlexRule Niche Players. — [Gartner abstract](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/7363830); [Quantexa press release](https://www.quantexa.com/press/quantexa-recognized-as-a-leader-in-2026-gartner-magic-quadranttm-for-decision-intelligence-platforms/); [Aera](https://www.aeratechnology.com/gartner-magic-quadrant-decision-intelligence-leader/) (search summary)
- ADRs: a 2023 IEEE Access mining study of GitHub found ADR adoption "still low" though rising yearly; an ICSA 2026 paper says adoption "remains inconsistent" and impact is "poorly understood"; an ECSA 2024 action-research study found ADRs improved cross-team cooperation and that where records are stored has "a massive influence" on perceived usefulness. — [Buchgeher et al., JKU](https://research.jku.at/en/publications/using-architecture-decision-records-in-open-source-projects-an-ms/); [ICSA 2026](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icsa-2026/icsa-2026-papers/34/Architecture-Decision-Records-Adoption-Impact-and-Developer-Engagement-in-Open-Sou); [ECSA 2024](https://conf.researchr.org/details/ecsa-2024/ecsa-2024-research-papers/9/Introducing-Architecture-Decision-Records-in-Practice-An-Action-Research-Study) (search summary)
- Practitioner accounts (opinion, no measured rates): ADRs are "written carefully for several months, and then quietly abandoned"; records kept in Confluence/Notion away from the codebase go unread; heavyweight approval "kills adoption". — [Java Code Geeks](https://www.javacodegeeks.com/?p=143468); [H. Konishi](https://hidekazu-konishi.com/entry/architecture_decision_records_templates_and_operations.html); [Helixgate](https://helixgate-web.pages.dev/blog/implement-adr-governance.html) (search summary)
- Granola, a product whose records are a by-product of work already happening (meetings), raised $125M at a $1.5B valuation in Mar 2026 (Series C, Index Ventures), up 6x from $250M under a year earlier, and is repositioning as an enterprise "context" platform with team workspaces and APIs. — [The Next Web, 25 Mar 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/granola-series-c-meeting-ai-enterprise-context); [Implicator](https://implicator.ai/granola-raises-125-million-at-1-5-billion-valuation-for-ai-notetaker/) (search summary)

### Inferences
- Enterprises pay for decision automation at scale (credit, fraud, supply chain) and for passive capture of context (meetings). They have not paid much for humans deliberately logging judgment calls and scoring them later. Cloverpop is the closest precedent to a "team Grill" and its trajectory is the key disconfirming datum.
- ADR evidence generalises: decision records survive only when they live where the work lives and cost almost nothing to write. Records that require a separate destination or ceremony decay.
- The Granola contrast suggests the valuable framing is "record as exhaust of something people already want" (a verdict, a meeting note), not "record as discipline".

- No market-size figure for decision intelligence was retrieved (Gartner report is paywalled). No Cloverpop pricing, customer count or ARR found. Nothing retrieved on decision-log usage in Notion/Confluence/Linear or on Amazon-style decision docs.
- The JetBrains 2024 figure "26% of engineering teams have a formal ADR process" appeared only in an unverified DEV Community post.

## 3. Does feedback on past decisions improve future decisions? Effect sizes

### Takeaway
The best evidence (Good Judgment Project) shows modest, real gains from brief training (6-11% Brier improvement) in a setting with frequent, clearly resolved questions; evidence that outcome feedback alone improves calibration is mixed, including a 2025 null result. Nothing shows that a few dozen idiosyncratic personal decisions reviewed months later make someone measurably better.

### Cited Findings
- In GJP's four-year tournament, training of one hour or less (probabilistic reasoning, outside view, bias avoidance) improved Brier scores by 6-11% over controls, with effects lasting at least a year; the authors call this a likely lower bound for more intensive interventions. — [Chang, Chen, Mellers & Tetlock 2016, Judgment and Decision Making](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/123EB18425391D05FA6581FDBB3F309F/S1930297500004599a.pdf/developing-expert-political-judgment-the-impact-of-training-and-practice-on-judgmental-accuracy-in-geopolitical-forecasting-tournaments.pdf) (search summary)
- Feedback studies are mixed: a 2000 study found performance feedback reduced overconfidence while environmental feedback improved discrimination, with neither transferring to the other; a 2005 study found people rarely consulted an available decision aid and stayed highly overconfident; a medical study found feedback did not improve physicians' calibration on harder cases; Martin & Mandel (2025) found no calibration improvement from either performance-feedback or outcome-feedback training versus control. — [Training to Improve Calibration (CSULB copy)](https://home.csulb.edu/~cwallis/382/certainty/overconfidence/Training%20to%20Improve%20Calibration.pdf); [Sieck & Arkes 2005](https://www.globalcognition.org/articles/sieck-jbdm05-decision-aids-overconfidence.pdf); [residents' self-assessment study, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8938348/); [Martin & Mandel 2025, Futures & Foresight Science](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/fufsci/v7y2025i1ne199.html) (search summary)
- A 2023 review cautions that over 60% of psychology meta-analyses overstate evidence for an effect and over 50% overstate its size, relevant to any claimed debiasing effect. — [Royal Society Open Science 2023, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10320355) (search summary)
- Simple statistical recalibration of forecasts works mechanically: Metaculus (May 2026) found Platt scaling improved bots' Brier scores by 0.016 on binary questions. — [Metaculus roundup via LessWrong, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say)

### Inferences
- The honest product claim is narrow: tracking shows a person their over/under-confidence; it is not proven to make their decisions better. GJP's gains came from hundreds of crisp questions with fast resolution and scoring, the opposite of personal strategic decisions.
- The Platt-scaling result points to a use of outcome data that does not rely on changing human behaviour: the system can adjust its own stated confidence (or the user's) using their history. That is a more defensible value than "you will become better calibrated".

### Gaps
- No pooled effect size for outcome feedback or calibration training was found. No source retrieved on premortem efficacy or on "resulting"/outcome bias effect sizes; the commonly quoted premortem figure (prospective hindsight raising reasons identified by about 30%, Mitchell, Russo & Pennington 1989) is from my memory and was not verified this session.

## 4. Data moat mechanics: when is a longitudinal outcome dataset a moat?

### Takeaway
The standard critique (a16z, 2019) is that data advantages are scale effects with diminishing value and rising cost per unique datum, not network effects. Applied to Grill, the data volume needed is modest for showing a user rough calibration but large for ranking judge models, and the binding constraint is resolved outcomes, not logged decisions.

### Cited Findings
- Casado & Lauten argue most "data network effects" are scale effects; the cost of adding unique data often rises as the corpus grows, marginal value falls, and where the curve flattens varies by domain. — [a16z, "The Empty Promise of Data Moats", 2019](https://a16z.com/the-empty-promise-of-data-moats/) (search summary)
- Later commentary: moats "are never about data alone" (Choudary 2025, paywalled); a16z's own later essay uses vLex (law) and OpenEvidence (medicine) as cases where proprietary corpora matter. — [Platforms Substack, "The 'data moats' fallacy"](https://platforms.substack.com/p/the-data-moats-fallacy); [a16z "Fruits of the Walled Garden"](https://a16z.com/fruits-of-the-walled-garden/) (search summary; arguments not read in full)
- Sample-size reference points: ForecastBench will not list a submission until at least 225 dataset questions have resolved, and takes about 50 days for tournament placement; with hundreds of questions per model, top systems still differ from superforecasters at p of 0.14-0.41, i.e. not separable. — [FRI, 16 Jul 2026](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-models-have-likely-reached-parity)
- The most rigorous superforecaster-parity paper's live test (n = 64) was "too small to distinguish it from market consensus". Metaculus head-to-head tournaments needed hundreds of questions per quarter to get significant pro-vs-bot differences (e.g. p = 0.079 in Q4 2024, p = 0.001 in Q1 2025). — [Metaculus roundup, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say)
- Clinical prediction literature: calibration tests are highly sensitive to sample size; one closed-form estimate required about 930-950 cases for a calibration-slope confidence interval of width 0.1; Brier scores vary widely at small n. — [PubMed 17419960](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17419960/); [arXiv 2305.03780](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.03780) (search summary; clinical setting, not personal forecasting)

### Inferences
- (a) Personal calibration: a statistically stable calibration curve needs hundreds of resolved predictions; a person logging two or three real decisions a week with partial look-back completion would take years. With 20-50 resolved items a product can honestly show only a coarse signal (for example "of the calls you rated 80%+, 6 of 10 worked"). This is my arithmetic from the sample-size sources, not a published threshold.
- (b) Judge evaluation: separating two judge models whose accuracy differs by a few Brier points needs on the order of several hundred to a few thousand resolved, comparable verdicts per arm, and the outcome labels are self-reported, delayed, selected (people look back on memorable outcomes) and confounded (the verdict changes what the person does). So the aggregate dataset is useful for coarse questions (does any outside challenge beat none; is a judge systematically too harsh) well before it can rank near-equal models.
- Per the a16z logic, raw volume is not the moat. What is distinctive is that the labels are private real-world outcomes of ordinary work decisions, which labs cannot scrape and public forecasting benchmarks do not cover. That is a real but narrow asset whose value depends on look-back completion rate and on having the right to use it (see section 6).
- The stronger user-side lock-in is not statistical. It is the accumulated personal record (switching cost, like notes history), which exists from the first saved decision and does not require calibration significance.

### Gaps
- No published minimum-n for individual forecaster calibration was found. Nothing retrieved on how Clari/Gong or clinical decision-support vendors used outcome labels to prove efficacy; treat those precedents as unresearched here.

## 5. LLM forecasting vs superforecasters, 2025-2026

### Takeaway
By mid-2026 the best AI forecasting systems were statistically indistinguishable from the (2024-vintage) superforecaster median on ForecastBench, while Metaculus's live head-to-heads still had human pro teams ahead of every bot through spring 2026. Parity on generic forecasting is arriving within Grill's planning horizon, so "our judge predicts better" cannot be the moat; private context and ground-truth outcomes are what remain scarce.

### Cited Findings
- ForecastBench, 4 Mar 2026: superforecasters Brier 0.086 vs best LLMs 0.103 (CassiAI ensemble and Grok 4.20 Preview); extrapolated parity May 2027 (95% CI Apr 2026 - May 2028). — [FRI, Brier Index post](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/introducing-the-brier-index)
- ForecastBench, 16 Jul 2026: "AI models have likely reached parity with superforecasters". Cassi AI top, xAI and Google DeepMind in the same group (one-sided p-values 0.41, 0.15-0.16, 0.14 against equal accuracy); Cassi is the first system above the superforecaster median on market questions; 17 submissions rank above superforecasters on the preliminary dataset-question leaderboard. Caveats: superforecaster forecasts were last collected in 2024 and are statistically extrapolated; rankings are stochastic; data fit parity, not clear outperformance. A fresh superforecaster round was planned for fall 2026. — [FRI, 16 Jul 2026](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-models-have-likely-reached-parity)
- Cassi's winning method is scaffolding, not a single model: generate sub-questions and searches, retrieve and filter recent context, ensemble several models, then have another LLM review the reasoning. — [FRI, 16 Jul 2026](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-models-have-likely-reached-parity)
- Metaculus (roundup of 8 Jul 2026): pro forecaster teams beat bot teams in every head-to-head to date (lead 11.3 peer points in Q3 2024, 8.9 in Q4 2024, 17.7 in Q1 2025, 20.03 in Q2 2025, p = 0.00001); on the May 2026 FutureEval leaderboard the best model trails pros by about 16 peer-score points. Best bots place in the top 3-6% of the open Metaculus Cup (e.g. 33rd of 1,130 in spring 2026). Extrapolated timelines for beating top humans: late 2026 to mid-2027. The authors consider superforecaster-parity papers unproven because of possible leakage in backtests. — [LessWrong linkpost, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say)
- Other Metaculus findings relevant to judge design: number of research sources correlated with bot score (r = 0.42); 34 of 39 bot makers used a frontier model; automated prompt engineering showed a null result on a strong model (o3, n = 278); no search provider had a consistent edge. — [same roundup](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say)
- Good Judgment Inc. disputes the headline, saying the best LLM still lags the best human forecasters by 40% and that each layer of statistical bridging adds uncertainty (search summary; page returned 403 when fetched). — [Good Judgment blog](https://goodjudgment.com/category/good-judgment-inc/page/1)

### Inferences
- For a product whose judge predicts whether a plan will work: frontier models with retrieval and ensembling will be at or near top-human level on public, well-specified questions by 2027. Prompt cleverness adds little on strong models (the o3 null result); information access and ensembling add more. A judge-quality moat built on prompts is therefore weak, and the main chat model will itself be a competent forecaster.
- What benchmarks do not measure is forecasting on private, under-specified plans where the decisive facts are in the user's head or company. There, accuracy is limited by context and by absence of ground truth. A product holding the user's past decisions and outcomes has an input the labs' models lack. This supports the record as an asset, conditional on outcomes actually being captured.
- Independence (a second model from a different company) is an ensembling/diversity argument; ForecastBench's top entry being a multi-model ensemble with an LLM reviewer is consistent with that design, though it does not prove that a single outside judge beats self-review.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve results of FRI's fall 2026 superforecaster re-baseline, so whether parity holds against fresh human forecasts is unknown as of this note. Mantic and FutureSearch results were not retrieved individually. I found no benchmark of LLMs predicting outcomes of private business or personal plans.

## 6. Privacy tension: can you have a data moat on data you promise not to read?

### Takeaway
The cautionary precedent is Rewind/Limitless: a privacy-first personal-record product whose users lost the product and, in some regions, access when it was acquired by Meta in December 2025. I found no retrieved evidence of a product that both promises not to read user data and derives an aggregate data moat from it; the two goals trade off unless aggregation is explicit opt-in.

### Cited Findings
- Meta acquired Limitless (maker of Rewind) in early Dec 2025. The Rewind Mac app's screen and audio capture was disabled from 19 Dec 2025; Pendant sales ended with support promised for at least a year; service was withdrawn in the EU, UK, Brazil, China, Israel, South Korea and Turkey, with users given until 19 Dec 2025 to download data; continuing users had to accept new terms bringing data under Meta. — [9to5Mac, 5 Dec 2025](https://9to5mac.com/2025/12/05/rewind-limitless-meta-acquisition/); [heise](https://heise.de/-11105985) (search summary)
- Granola reached a $1.5B valuation in Mar 2026 on meeting records and is moving to enterprise team workspaces and APIs, i.e. value from the customer's own corpus rather than from cross-customer aggregation. — [The Next Web, 25 Mar 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/granola-series-c-meeting-ai-enterprise-context) (search summary)

### Inferences
- Two different "moats" are being conflated. (1) Per-user/per-team lock-in from an accumulated private record is compatible with strong privacy and user-owned export; it is what Granola-type products monetise. (2) A cross-user outcome dataset for evaluating judges requires the maker to read, or at least compute over, content and outcomes, which conflicts with a "we don't read your decisions" stance unless users opt in, ideally to a minimal schema (verdict type, stated confidence, outcome label, judge/model id) rather than decision text.
- An opt-in, content-free aggregate is enough for the judge-evaluation purpose in section 4 (it needs verdict, confidence, model and outcome, not the plan text) and keeps the privacy promise intact. The cost is a smaller, self-selected sample.
- The Limitless outcome shows the trust risk attached to a sensitive personal corpus: users of decision records about their boss, career or company strategy will weigh acquisition and policy-change risk. Export and deletion guarantees are part of the product, not a nicety.

### Gaps
- Nothing was retrieved on Obsidian, Day One, Reflect or 1Password user/revenue numbers, or on documented opt-in aggregate-telemetry designs and their opt-in rates. The claim that privacy-first tools can build large businesses is plausible from general knowledge but unsourced in this session.

## 7. Enterprise/regulatory pull for independent challenge and audit trails of AI-assisted decisions

### Takeaway
Regulatory pressure exists but has just been pushed out and narrowed: the EU's high-risk obligations (including human oversight and logging) now start 2 Dec 2027, and the US bank model-risk guidance that replaced SR 11-7 in April 2026 is non-binding and reportedly leaves generative and agentic AI out of scope. AI governance is a real but still small budget line (Gartner: about $492M in 2026) sold by enterprise vendors at custom prices; it is not a near-term pull for a prosumer second-opinion tool.

### Cited Findings
- EU Digital Omnibus on AI: adopted by co-legislators around 29 Jun - 8 Jul 2026 (sources differ), in force 27 Jul 2026 as Regulation 2026/1744. Stand-alone (Annex III) high-risk obligations move to 2 Dec 2027; product-embedded (Annex I) to 2 Aug 2028. Article 50(2) watermarking and new prohibitions apply from 2 Dec 2026; most transparency duties are not delayed. — [Loyens & Loeff](https://www.loyensloeff.com/insights/news--events/news/eu-ai-act-key-amendments-adopted-under-digital-omnibus-package/); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/eu-digital-omnibus-ai-enters-force); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/fr-FR/Insights/2026/05/EUs-Digital-Omnibus-on-AI-7-Key-Changes-You-Need-to-Know); [AKOS FAQ](https://www.akos-rs.si/en/umetna-inteligenca/explore/frequently-asked-questions-and-answers/digital-omnibus-on-artificial-intelligence) (search summary)
- EU AI Act deployer duties for high-risk systems: assign human oversight to people with competence, training and authority (Art. 26(2)); keep automatically generated logs under their control for at least six months (Art. 26(6)); providers must design in oversight measures (Art. 14(3)). — [artificialintelligenceact.eu, Article 26](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/); [RTR deployer obligations](https://www.rtr.at/rtr/service/ki-servicestelle/ai-act/Deployer_obligations.en.html) (search summary)
- US banking: the Fed issued SR 26-2 on 17 Apr 2026, superseding SR 11-7 (2011) and SR 21-8; it is "expected to be most relevant to banking organizations with over $30 billion in total assets" and is risk-based. — [Federal Reserve SR 26-2](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr2602.htm)
- Commentary on SR 26-2: it is principles-based guidance, not an enforceable rule; its scope covers traditional models and "non-generative, non-agentic AI", with generative and agentic AI needing other governance controls ("SR 26-2 Regulates Your Models, Not Your AI Agents"), though one consultancy argues exclusion does not mean nothing applies; American Banker says it "leaves many questions unanswered". — [Sia Partners](https://www.sia-partners.com/en/insights/publications/sr-11-7-vs-sr-26-2-model-risk-management-modernization); [Cimcon](https://cimcon.com/?p=7908); [Elevate](https://elevateconsult.com/insights/model-risk-management-framework-rebuild-revised-guidance/); [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/opinion/regulators-guidance-on-model-risk-leaves-many-questions-unanswered) (search summary)
- AI governance market: Gartner projects AI governance platform spending of $492M in 2026 and over $1B by 2030, and published an inaugural Magic Quadrant for AI Governance Platforms on 16 Jun 2026. Credo AI and Holistic AI both sell on custom/quote pricing (one guide says Holistic AI has a free starter tier). Credo AI treats agents as governed entities but, per a competitor's guide, listed runtime enforcement "at the point of use" as a roadmap item in May 2026; Holistic AI markets runtime "Safeguard" filtering and "Guardian Agents". — [Kovrr comparison guide](https://www.kovrr.com/blog-post/top-ai-governance-platforms-2026-comparison-guide); [Kosmoy](https://kosmoy.com/resources/blog/holistic-ai-alternatives/); [Credo AI comparison page](https://www.credo.ai/lp/holistic-ai-vs-credo-ai) (search summary; vendor and competitor marketing, low confidence)

### Inferences
- "A documented independent challenge of an AI recommendation" maps conceptually onto EU Art. 14 oversight (including awareness of over-reliance on AI output) and onto the model-risk idea of effective challenge, but no retrieved source shows a regulator or auditor asking for a second-model challenge specifically. The requirement is human oversight and logs, which incumbents and the AI platforms' own audit logs can satisfy.
- A general-purpose assistant used for plans and memos is typically not a high-risk system under the Act, so the audit-trail argument applies only where Grill would be embedded in Annex III workflows (hiring, credit, education), and then only from Dec 2027.
- The gap in SR 26-2 around generative/agentic AI means banks must invent their own controls for agents. That is an opening for "independent challenge" evidence, but the buyers are large institutions purchasing through governance platforms at enterprise prices, with security review and procurement cycles; a per-seat prosumer tool is not the natural vendor. Realistic route, if any: be the challenge-and-record component that exports into those systems.
- Net: the audit-trail moat is a 2027-2028 option, not a 2026 demand signal.

### Gaps
- I could not open the SR 26-2 attachment, so whether "effective challenge" language is retained, and the exact wording on generative/agentic AI, are unverified (the Fed cover page mentions neither; one source gives a 2027 effective date). Nothing retrieved on NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001 or SOC 2 demand for challenge records, on agent approval/guardrail vendor pricing, or on actual contract values for Credo AI/Holistic AI. Automation-bias wording of Art. 14(4)(b) is from my memory, not a fetched source.

## 8. Willingness to pay: prosumer thinking tools, AI add-ons, MCP connectors, "boss-facing work"

### Takeaway
AI subscription apps monetise early but churn fast, and paid standalone MCP connectors have almost no verified revenue; money in the connector channel goes to vendors with an existing paid product behind the connector. I found no evidence either way on "boss-facing work" as a paying segment.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat 2026 (via press): AI apps' median trial-to-paid conversion 8.5% vs 5.6% for non-AI; about 41% more revenue per payer over a year; but about 30% faster churn, 12-month annual-plan retention 21.1% vs 30.7%, monthly-plan retention 6.1% vs 9.5%. — [TechCrunch, 10 Mar 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows); [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/blog/revenuecat-data-shows-subscription-app-growth-concentrating-at-the-top-news) (search summary)
- An unverified developer post citing the same report gives 2.1% conversion for freemium vs 10.7% for hard paywalls. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/zaraai_0b75675ddc9204c716/ai-apps-generate-41-more-revenue-per-user-but-lose-79-of-annual-subscribers-before-month-13-485a) (unverified)
- MCP monetization (blog-level evidence): about 17,000+ indexed servers in mid-2026 with only 3-6% carrying any monetization; another post claims 95%+ earn nothing; most paid ones are existing SaaS products exposed via MCP (Stripe, Notion, Linear); "entry into the paid MCP tier still requires either a paid underlying product, a vertical specialization, or backing from a known commercial vendor". — [DEV: State of MCP monetization 2026](https://dev.to/kirothebot/the-state-of-mcp-monetization-in-2026-where-builders-actually-get-paid-34k9); [DEV: N. Fainstein](https://dev.to/nicolas_fainstein/the-state-of-mcp-monetization-in-2026-28i4) (search summary; self-published, no dataset)
- PitchBook (Morningstar investor Q&A, 2026) is piloting premium connector access that still requires at least one full platform licence, with connector-only seats as an add-on. — [Morningstar investor Q&A](https://shareholders.morningstar.com/investor-resources/investor-qa/qa-details/2026/How-are-PitchBook-Model-Context-Protocol-MCP-Connectors-monetized-today-if-at-all--2026-cy9M2BxfO7/default.aspx) (search summary)

### Inferences
- If freemium conversion for AI apps is in the low single digits and annual retention near 20%, a Pro tier whose value (history, look-back reminders) accrues slowly is exposed: users may churn before the record is large enough to matter. The record's switching cost has to be felt within the first month or two, for example by showing past verdicts and due look-backs immediately rather than a calibration score that needs a year of data.
- The connector channel is a distribution surface, not yet a business model. The pattern that works is a paid product with its own account and stored state, reached through the connector. Grill's saved history is the natural "product behind the connector".

### Gaps
- No reliable data found on typical price points for prosumer thinking tools, on conversion for chat-embedded add-ons specifically, or on demand for tools aimed at work sent to a manager. The "boss-facing work" segment is unevidenced in this research; it needs primary user research rather than desk research.

## Overall assessment for the report writer (inference, drawing on all sections)

- Supporting the candidate moat: private real-world outcomes are the one input labs do not have and public benchmarks do not cover (sections 4, 5); generic forecasting skill is commoditising by 2027, which shifts value toward context and ground truth (section 5); capture inside the chat removes the friction that sank predecessors (section 1); a personal/team record creates switching costs without needing statistical scale (section 4).
- Against it: no decision-journal or prediction-tracking product has shown paid demand or published retention, and the category runs on grants (section 1); the closest enterprise precedent stayed small (section 2); outcome feedback's benefit to the user is modest and contested (section 3); calibration and judge-ranking need hundreds to thousands of resolved outcomes, and resolution is the historically unsolved step (sections 1, 4); a cross-user dataset conflicts with a no-read privacy promise unless opt-in (section 6); regulatory pull is delayed to Dec 2027 and aimed at enterprise governance buyers (section 7).
- The single metric that decides whether the moat is real is the look-back completion rate on recorded decisions. Nothing in the public record tells us what it will be.
