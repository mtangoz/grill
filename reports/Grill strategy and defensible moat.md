# Grill's moat must move from judge to record

Grill's current moat thesis does not hold as written, and the evidence to replace it does not exist yet. The strategy rests the anti-copy defence on a judge that is "measurably better than a same-company critique, shown with published evals", but no such measurement exists in the repo, and the outside research says the "different company" element is the smallest and fastest-shrinking part of what a second opinion provides. Most of the measurable benefit comes from a fresh context and an opposing brief, which any lab can ship with its own model, and several already have. What a host lab cannot copy is narrower: a guarantee that the judge shares no memory, system prompt or commercial incentive with the assistant that wrote the plan, and a decision record with outcomes that spans every assistant a person uses. That record is the only asset here that compounds and that a better model does not supply, but it is unproven: Grill has **1 real user and 0 evidenced look-backs**, and no decision-journal product has ever shown a paying market. The recommendation is to keep cross-company independence as a trust guarantee, stop claiming it as a quality edge until it is measured, and spend the next quarter producing two numbers that decide the strategy: the measured increment of a cross-company judge over a same-model fresh-context critique, and the look-back completion rate among real outside users. Nothing else on the roadmap matters until those exist, and both depend on getting users, which is currently blocked on the owner's own approvals.

A note on labels. Repo facts are as written on 2026-11-13 and were not checked against the live service. Claims marked *(secondhand)* come from press, blogs, abstracts or search summaries that the researchers did not read in full. Claims marked *(inference)* are reasoning, not sourced findings.

## Three moat legs are claimed and only one is built

The strategy document names three defences. The first is enforced cross-company independence ("independence is the product"). The second is a measured quality edge: "Copyable is fine. Unmeasured is not." The third is the owned record: "The record is the product; the verdict is how it starts" ([docs/STRATEGY.md](../docs/STRATEGY.md)). Only the first exists in code and tests, and it has holes.

**The quality leg is unmeasured.** The evals are regression floors on 29 synthetic cases that the README itself calls "not a statistically powered benchmark". The `decorrelatedRate` metric checks only that the judge was routed outside the author's family; it compares nothing. No eval scores are committed, CI has no key and prints `SKIPPED: no judge ran`, and the changelog admits that before a recent fix "every eval run since the workflow was added passed without reaching a judge" ([evals/README.md](../evals/README.md), [CHANGELOG.md](../CHANGELOG.md)). The one blind A/B that was run tested a prompt change, and the candidate lost (9 of 15, p = 0.30) ([evals/verdict-quality/README.md](../evals/verdict-quality/README.md)). That shows discipline. It does not show product value. Publishing eval results is a roadmap item marked "not met" ([docs/ROADMAP.md](../docs/ROADMAP.md)).

**The independence leg leaks on its own terms.** In hosted chat an unrecognised client "excludes nobody unless the call names an author"; Cursor excludes nobody on purpose; an unrecognised installed client excludes Anthropic whoever the real author was; the paste route only warns. The fix in flight adds a disclosure line and leaves "who is excluded" unchanged ([docs/STRATEGY.md](../docs/STRATEGY.md), [docs/ROADMAP.md](../docs/ROADMAP.md), [docs/HOSTED.md](../docs/HOSTED.md)). The strategy says the platform defence "holds only if Grill says it clearly and enforces it on every route". It is not enforced on every route.

**The record leg has no outside evidence.** As of 2026-11-09 the count is 1 real user, 0 evidenced look-backs, and 4 hosted accounts of which 3 are maintainer or test accounts. The usage figures that inform pricing come from the maintainer's own company. The owner's own grills in the October review "recorded no confidence or prediction, so there was nothing to score" ([docs/STRATEGY.md](../docs/STRATEGY.md), [docs/PRD-lookback-reminders.md](../docs/PRD-lookback-reminders.md)). The look-back persona study was a simulation with invented personas ([docs/lookback-icp-sim-2026-11-07.md](../docs/lookback-icp-sim-2026-11-07.md)).

Grill's hard promises also remove the usual moats by design. The decision-handling code is MIT "for good", the prompt is open, no model is pinned or fine-tuned, decisions are not read or reused, and data partnerships are ruled out "at any price" ([docs/PRINCIPLES.md](../docs/PRINCIPLES.md), [docs/DATA-LEDGER.md](../docs/DATA-LEDGER.md)). These are sound trust choices, and the open-source record supports never relicensing: Elastic and Redis both paid for it *(secondhand)* ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/forks-clouds-and-the-new-economics-of-open-source-licensing/)). The consequence is that nothing in the code is defensible, and a cross-user dataset of decisions is off the table. Any durable asset has to be per-user state, an opt-in content-free aggregate, a published track record, or the name.

Trust is the asset, so the repo's internal contradictions are strategic problems, not tidying. The README keeps the promise that only an approved write-up leaves the machine while the hosted connector tells the assistant to send the decision "and not to wait for an OK". Telemetry is opt-in by principle while the Monday email is "on unless they turn it off". The hosted relay "breaks one sentence of today's promise" by the PRD's own admission ([docs/PRINCIPLES.md](../docs/PRINCIPLES.md), [docs/PRD-hosted-pro.md](../docs/PRD-hosted-pro.md), [docs/DATA-LEDGER.md](../docs/DATA-LEDGER.md)).

## A different company is the smallest part of the benefit

The research separates three things a second opinion provides, and they do not age the same way.

**The problem Grill attacks is real and large.** On open-ended advice, models validate the user 72% of the time against 22% for humans *(abstract-level)*, and they tell both sides of the same conflict that they are not at fault in 48% of pairs ([ELEPHANT](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13995v2)). The part closest to "is this plan sound", accepting the user's premise, got worse from GPT-4o to GPT-5 (0.34 to 0.45 excess over humans) while other measures improved ([ELEPHANT](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13995v2)). Labs report 70 to 85% reductions on their own metrics ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/protecting-well-being-of-users)), but these are self-reported, and independent 2026 benchmarks do not agree with each other *(secondhand)*.

**Most of the fix comes from framing and role, with the same model.** Rewriting the query in the third person cut GPT-4o's validation excess from 0.76 to 0.32, using the same model ([ELEPHANT](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13995v2)). An opposing debater raised judge accuracy from 48% to 76% with the same model family on both sides *(abstract-level)* ([Khan et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.06782v4)). A lab can deliver both with a "review this cold, argue against it" button.

**The cross-company increment is real, small and shrinking.** When two models are both wrong they pick the same wrong answer 60% of the time against 33% by chance, and being from the same company adds only a few points on top of that baseline ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07962v1)). Error similarity rises with capability ([Goel et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04313v2)). Nine frontier judges from seven families carry about two independent votes *(abstract only)* ([Kohli](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29800)). The best supporting result is that cross-family verification beats same-family, which beats self-checking, with the gap widening for stronger solvers, but it was measured on open models up to 72B only ([Lu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02304v2)). One 2026 paper reportedly puts the cross-model gain over same-context self-review at about 1.5 points of F1; the researchers could not open it, so treat it as *unverified* ([ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/415153376_When_Does_a_Second_Model_Help_Cross-Model_Review_in_LLM_Verification)).

Two cautions follow for the product. First, judge quality outranks judge difference: mixing different models lost to six samples from the single best model by 6.6 points on one benchmark ([Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00674v1)). A weaker judge chosen for being "different" can make the verdict worse, and Grill's judge is whatever OpenRouter's Auto Router serves under a policy OpenRouter changes without notice ([OpenRouter docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/routers/auto-router.md)). Second, a council is not the answer to improving models; the panel evidence says extra judges add almost nothing over the best one.

Every one of these results comes from multiple-choice, maths, NLI or summarisation tasks. **Nothing in the literature tests Grill's configuration on open-ended decisions with outcomes**, and no clean ablation of model versus context versus role exists. The attribution above (tens of points for context and role, single digits for vendor) is the researchers' synthesis, not a measured decomposition. This is a gap Grill can fill cheaply with its existing blind-pair harness, and the result would be publishable whichever way it falls.

So Grill's strategy principle "get better when models get better" is half right. Routing to the best available outside model does improve the judge for free. But the same improvement makes the host assistant a competent critic of its own work, and forecasting skill is commoditising: the best AI systems were statistically level with the superforecaster median on ForecastBench by July 2026, with caveats about the stale human baseline ([FRI](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-models-have-likely-reached-parity)). What survives is structural *(inference)*: the judge did not write the plan, has no relationship with the author, and is not paid by the author's vendor. That is a trust and governance claim, not an accuracy claim, and Grill should sell it as one.

## The critical second pass is already being absorbed

The cross-company idea is no longer unique. OpenAI ships an official Codex plugin for Claude Code with an adversarial-review command, usable on a free ChatGPT account, at 35.1k stars ([GitHub](https://github.com/openai/codex-plugin-cc)). PAL MCP offers `consensus` and `challenge` tools free at 11.8k stars ([GitHub](https://github.com/BeehiveInnovations/pal-mcp-server)). Microsoft 365 Copilot has a Critique mode in which GPT drafts and Claude reviews, in preview ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/362805/microsoft-gpt-claude-work-together-ai-research)). Grill's own register lists Triall and council-ai.app with the same hosted-MCP delivery shape and concludes that "a judge from a different vendor is becoming a common idea" ([docs/COMPETITORS.md](../docs/COMPETITORS.md)). The Grill repo has 1 star ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/mtangoz/grill)). Developers will not pay for this layer.

The same-vendor version is native too. Anthropic's advisor lets Claude consult a second model "before committing to an approach", describes Opus-reviews-Opus as "an independent check", and accepts only Anthropic models ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/advisor.md)). That confirms the one thing a lab will not ship: a reviewer from a rival. No lab has said so; it is an inference from product design. Whether users care about that difference is untested, and the one large study on preference cuts the wrong way: people rated sycophantic and non-sycophantic AI as equally objective and preferred the sycophantic one *(secondhand)* ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/28/stanford-study-outlines-dangers-of-asking-ai-chatbots-for-personal-advice/)).

The likely sequence *(inference)* is that a free same-vendor "challenge this" button satisfies casual users, neutral clients such as Copilot and Perplexity make cross-vendor critique standard, and Grill is left with what neither provides: a record across hosts, with outcomes.

Distribution is rented and the rent terms constrain the business. ChatGPT's rules say plugins "must not display subscription plans, initiate new subscriptions, or promote upgrades" and must not relay queries to third-party APIs "without proper authorization" ([OpenAI guidelines](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/app-submission-guidelines)). Claude's directory policy says software "must not persistently store or use conversation content to build user profiles" ([Anthropic policy](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13145358-anthropic-software-directory-policy)). Neither forbids calling a rival's model, but no ruling exists on a connector whose whole job is to do that. **The record, Grill's most plausible moat, sits exactly where platform policy is strictest**, and Pro can only be sold on grillyour.ai. Platforms also retire extension points on their own schedule: plugins in 2024, and Custom GPTs reportedly in 2026 *(secondhand)* ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/openai-pissed-off-developers-by-phasing-out-plugins-for-1851124124), [Virtualization Review](https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2026/09/28/openai-to-retire-custom-gpts-replace-them-with-plugins.aspx)). Being on several assistants with a direct account relationship is the hedge, and Grill already has that shape.

The real substitute is not a competitor. It is typing "argue the other side" into the same chat, against a 1 to 3 minute wait and per-app connector setup ([docs/HOSTED.md](../docs/HOSTED.md)). The host assistant also decides whether Grill is called, what is sent and how the verdict is relayed, and can answer "as Grill" without calling it ([MERGE-LOG.md](../MERGE-LOG.md)).

## The record compounds, but nobody has shown people come back

The moat frameworks converge on one test: if the model gets dramatically better tomorrow, is the product worth more or less ([SignalFire](https://signalfire.com/blog/why-ai-startups-should-optimize-for-permanence-not-moats))? They also converge on where durable value sits: per-customer context that "compounds, raising switching costs the longer the system is used", and compliance records that a third party requires ([Stanford Law paper](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Defensible-Moats-for-Vertical-AI-Application-Companies-in-a-New-Competitive-Landscape.pdf)). These are investor essays about venture-scale B2B companies. They apply to a free, solo, horizontal tool by analogy only.

Against that test, Grill's verdict is worth less as models improve and its record is worth the same or more. A dated record of what was decided, with what confidence, what test was proposed and what happened is not what platform memory produces, and outcome data cannot be backfilled by a later entrant *(inference)*. Capture inside the chat, as a by-product of getting a verdict, also removes the friction that users cited for abandoning PredictionBook ([Fatebook launch thread](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/yS3d46m23wRKDQobt/introducing-fatebook-the-fastest-way-to-make-and-track)).

The evidence against is heavier than the strategy document admits.

**No predecessor has shown a paying market.** The researchers found no published user counts, retention or revenue for any personal decision journal or prediction tracker; the durable ones run on grants and donations. The closest enterprise precedent, Cloverpop, stayed small for about a decade and was absorbed in 2021 *(secondhand)* ([Cloverpop blog](https://www.cloverpop.com/blog/cloverpop-was-acquired-by-clearbox-decisions)). AI subscription apps retain worse than others: 21.1% twelve-month retention on annual plans against 30.7% *(secondhand)* ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows)).

**The benefit to the user is modest and contested.** Brief training improved forecasting accuracy by 6 to 11% in a tournament with hundreds of crisp, fast-resolving questions *(abstract-level)* ([Chang et al.](https://dlab.sauder.ubc.ca/sjdm/journal/16/16511/jdm16511.html)). A 2025 study found no calibration gain from outcome feedback *(secondhand)* ([Martin & Mandel](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/fufsci/v7y2025i1ne199.html)). Nothing shows that a few dozen personal decisions reviewed months later make someone decide better. The strategy already concedes personal calibration takes "a year or more".

**The look-back is the historically unsolved step.** Delayed feedback, the ego cost of recording a miss and ambiguous outcomes are structural. No source shows a product that solved them. The look-back completion rate decides whether the record exists at all, and Grill's baseline is "0 of 0".

**Third-party demand for records is real but not near.** Independence businesses last when someone other than the decider requires the check. Consumer Reports is the clean precedent, paid by the people who rely on it *(secondhand)* ([Nonprofit Quarterly](https://nonprofitquarterly.org/consumer-reports-a-nonprofit-icon-with-business-model-questions/)). Gartner expects agents that oversee other agents to take 10 to 15% of the agentic AI market by 2030 ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-11-gartner-predicts-that-guardian-agents-will-capture-10-15-percent-of-the-agentic-ai-market-by-2030)). But the EU's high-risk obligations now start on 2 December 2027 *(secondhand)* ([Loyens & Loeff](https://www.loyensloeff.com/insights/news--events/news/eu-ai-act-key-amendments-adopted-under-digital-omnibus-package/)), the buyers are enterprise governance teams, and no source shows a regulator asking for a second-model challenge. No evidence was found either way that individuals will pay for an independent decision check.

The one data asset compatible with Grill's promises is the opt-in, content-free aggregate that [LEARNING.md](../LEARNING.md) already designs: verdict type, stated confidence, judge family and outcome label, never the decision text. It is small and self-selected, and ranking near-equal judges needs hundreds to thousands of resolved outcomes *(inference from benchmark sample sizes)*. It can still answer coarse questions early, such as whether the judge is systematically too harsh, and it is the only route to a published track record, which is what turns a neutrality claim into something a new entrant cannot copy.

## Recommendation: produce two numbers before building anything else

Hold the position, change the claim, and measure. Keep cross-company independence, because it is true counter-positioning, costs almost nothing and is the one thing a lab will not copy. Stop presenting it as a quality edge. Present it as a guarantee: no shared memory, no shared system prompt, no shared commercial interest. Put the long-term bet on the cross-assistant record with outcomes, and state plainly that the bet is a hypothesis until real users complete look-backs. Do not build a council, a calibration view, a second judge or more Pro features. The strategy's own self-assessment, "the product is ahead of the evidence", is correct, and the current roadmap is gated on a user count the owner's pending approvals control.

## Conclusion

The question "how does Grill stay useful as models improve" has a sharper form than the strategy gives it. Better models erode the verdict's edge and leave the record untouched, so the product should be built as though the verdict were a free sample: the reason someone records a decision at the moment they make it. The cross-company rule then earns its place as the reason to trust who is scoring the record, not as the reason the critique is better.

The uncomfortable part is that this reframing is also unproven, and the public record leans against it: nobody has made people come back to score their own decisions, and nobody has charged for it. Grill is in a position to find out cheaply, because capture is already frictionless and the instruments are already built. If the ablation shows a small cross-company increment and outside users do not complete look-backs, the honest outcome is a useful free tool with no moat, and that is better learned in a quarter than after another year of careful building for one user.
